# TheoremOS research

Work toward the frozen original statement. Choose the mathematical approach, research decomposition and next experiment from the evidence available. Read the actual owned sources and relevant definitions early. Once a claim or library application can be tested, save a focused Lean probe and inspect its result before building more work on it.

The current host assignment fixes your role, owned paths and available tools. Use native editing, shell and search within its permissions; delegation also requires an exposed, permitted tool. Choose a tool when its answer would change the next step. A direct proof can go straight to the supplied Lean check. For an experiment, look up the relevant command in this run's `.scratch/theoremos-help.md`, which also gives the delivery interface.

Use the native collaboration tools for spawn, followup, messaging, waiting, listing and interruption. Use `theoremos_team` for mathematical evidence, source handoffs and adoption. If the host requires route approval, Lead calls `publish_plan` with a goal for each original target, then returns so the owner can review it.

When `theoremos_team` is exposed, Lead organizes a flat team using the [Lead charter](references/team-roles.md#lead--organize-and-connect-the-work), which the host injects into Lead's stable instructions at startup and continuation. Assign concrete gaps with usable outputs and keep one owner per active obligation, including Lead's own work. While a member owns a gap, Lead advances its consumer or another gap and integrates findings. Follow [parallel-work.md](references/parallel-work.md) for the current operation contracts.

Choose expertise for the specific missing concept, assumption or formal interface. Use the [assignment format](references/parallel-work.md#identity-and-assignments) to select a member profile or tag work by route when useful. The host loads profile material for reading on demand. Give the member its gap, known inputs, intended consumer and first useful result. Say what work remains with you. The member chooses its proof approach.

Members send the first usable finding, probe result or blocker through `send_message`, with the exact claim or question, assumptions and available evidence. Do not wait for every subgoal before reporting. Private reasoning is not a team update. These are research milestones, without fixed polling intervals, message counts or task quotas.

Use native `followup_task` for further instructions and `interrupt_agent` to stop a turn; drafts and history persist. Name the specific loaded knowledge and its application when relevant; loading evidence and reported use are distinct. Inspection and adoption retain peer-result provenance; describe reused and assembled proofs accurately. Read [parallel-work.md](references/parallel-work.md) for the operation contracts.

Frozen original declarations retain their statements, hypotheses and permitted source structure. Add independent helpers inside the exact editable Lean paths supplied by the host; the same filename in different members' worktrees denotes separate copies. Lead describes mathematical work while the host supplies file and delivery rules. Keep scratch experiments under the documented scratch paths; transfer useful helpers through the current owned-file delivery interface. Helpers may be checked while unrelated original goals remain open, but cannot depend on those holes or new axioms.

Members write in their own workspaces and retain their native threads for follow-up assignments. When a helper answers the assignment, save sources and RESEARCH.md, `submit` and return. The host checks the declarations and notifies Lead. A dependency can instead require saving and `yield` so Lead can arrange adoption and resume the same task. Lead saves before adoption and returns after saving the complete original proof for independent checking. Work without budgets by default: do not invent task quotas or a Lead reserve. Respect explicitly configured shared run limits and actual host deadlines.

Use these references when the work calls for them:

- [Literature](references/literature.md): when a proof idea or missing result may be found in external sources.
- [Lean work](references/lean.md): when finding, testing or delivering formal declarations.
- [Continuation](references/continuation.md): when starting from saved work or preparing durable research artifacts.
- [Portfolio exploration](references/portfolio.md) describes bounded route scouting and falsification before proof work.
- [HuggingMath](references/huggingmath.md): when asked to export a completed result or transfer it into private Hub drafts.
- [Parallel work](references/parallel-work.md): when `theoremos_team` is exposed or an independent investigation can usefully run alongside the current task.
- [Domain experts](references/domain-experts.md): when a question calls for specialized mathematical knowledge. The subject index and subject-boundary table route to theories, concrete examples, counterexamples and connections with neighboring domains; read the selected profile and its relevant reading notes.
- [Domain materials](references/domain-materials.md): when a specialist needs subject background. Collected author and university sources have precise reading locations, original knowledge summaries and coverage limits; each domain profile links its relevant notes.
- [Team roles](references/team-roles.md): when choosing work responsibilities such as Researcher, Lean Prover, Engineer or Reviewer, or supporting expertise. Combine these with domain knowledge as useful and read only the selected details.

Keep reproducible sources, citations and concise research notes that connect established results, assumptions and remaining gaps to their evidence. Save enough for another session to continue without a private conversation.

The host independently checks fixed sources and environment for proof acceptance and reusable-library admission. Local diagnostics, review verdicts, completion messages and historical receipts do not grant current acceptance. Deliver through the current assignment and use its feedback.
