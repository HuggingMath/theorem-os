# Organize parallel research when it helps

Delegate only when delegation is exposed and permitted in this session. A useful independent assignment has a concrete question and an inspectable output, such as a source survey with hypothesis mapping, a counterexample search, an exact library declaration, or a separately checked candidate. Account for coordination and duplicated work; do not create tasks solely to keep agents busy.

Give an investigator the original statement and enough definitions and environment context to understand the assigned question. For an independent review, avoid supplying the answer it is expected to endorse. Retain disagreements and check the supporting artifacts before deciding what to integrate.

## Persistent proof-writing team

When the host exposes `theoremos_team`, Lead chooses the decomposition, assignments, route changes and adoption decisions. Children have isolated writable worktrees, the same pinned Skill and their own native threads. A later assignment continues that child's thread and saved sources. All agents share the run's time, turn and token allowances. The host enforces source ownership, process isolation, persistence and independent proof checks.

Organize a flat team around complementary gaps in the current proof effort. Direct discussion between members can clarify interfaces or challenge assumptions without passing every message through Lead. Explore an alternative route when its answer can change the team's decision; avoid independently repeating the whole original problem by default. Lead remains responsible for assignments, shared route changes and formal adoption, while each member chooses its mathematical approach.

### Identity and assignments

The durable identities are Lead and mathematical researchers who can write Lean. Lead may research and prove as well as coordinate. A member's `agentId`, workspace and thread persist; its expertise and work focus come from the current assignment and can change. Read [domain-experts.md](domain-experts.md) to select relevant mathematical knowledge, and [team-roles.md](team-roles.md) for responsibilities such as Researcher, Lean Prover, Lean Engineer and Reviewer. Read the selected domain section, including its concrete example and the linked reading passage relevant to the question. State the question and useful output; the expert chooses its reasoning and proof approach. Lead can combine profiles, reuse them across agents or specify another useful specialty, with the same source and acceptance rules.

Make `spawn.message` and `assign.message` self-contained: state any selected profile(s), the question or complete proposed declaration, relevant definitions and known inputs, intended use, expected output, and when the assignment has a useful answer. Profiles go in the message; there is no separate `role` tool parameter. Cite delivery IDs for checked inputs and research artifact IDs for saved advisory findings; include the mathematical content needed to begin. A child receives the frozen problem, its own saved work and the published team summary; it does not inherit Lead's conversation or a peer's mutable files. Private scratch paths alone do not transfer their contents. For an independent review, choose a member who did not produce the reviewed work and give the fixed claim and evidence without prescribing the verdict; follow the Reviewer profile for independence and blind meaning checks.

On a new assignment, read its task ID, current source/delivery baseline, previous task result, published research summary and addressed inbox. The current assignment replaces earlier objectives; current files and host feedback take priority over remembered claims. Retain useful drafts and rejected declarations for correction. If the objective or an interface is flawed, send Lead the precise issue, assumptions and supporting example or check; do not silently strengthen the hypotheses or switch the shared route. Messages supplement the assignment; `assign` queues more work. Lead uses `cancel` to withdraw a particular task or queue its replacement while keeping the member, and `stop` to retire the member permanently. A running task's cancellation request does not confirm writer shutdown; its replacement waits for that shutdown before starting in the same workspace and thread.

Communicate a useful reduction, blocking question or contradictory finding as soon as it affects another member's work. Include enough argument, exact declaration/type, assumptions and evidence references for the recipient to assess it. Optional `relatedTaskId` and `researchRevision` identify the task or published summary being discussed. The host records the sender's `sourceTaskId` and the research revision injected at its dispatch; that revision does not assert it has read later updates. A successful message call confirms durable inbox storage. Native notification is queued separately and is not a read acknowledgment. A local Lean check is advisory; use `submit` for transferable formal evidence. Lead can continue independent research while children work and use `wait` when progress depends on their arrivals.

When a peer dependency or Lead decision blocks the current task, save the draft and RESEARCH.md, call `yield(reason)` and return. The acknowledgment requests writer shutdown; the task becomes `paused` only after shutdown is confirmed. Lead may adopt a needed checked result and `resume(agentId, taskId)` to continue the same task, source baseline and thread. A normal return still finishes the investigation; use `yield` when the existing task should remain open. Do not wait indefinitely with an active writer, or call `yield` after requesting a formal submission.

Lead may give `spawn`/`assign` an optional `budget:{tokens?,turns?}` and use `budget` to replace a live task's allowance or `leadReserve:{tokens?,turns?}`. Task consumption is cumulative across yields and resumes; changing the allowance does not reset it. Supplied allowance/reserve objects replace their previous values, so include dimensions you intend to retain. An empty task allowance removes its local caps; an empty reserve releases the reserve. Tokens and turns still share the original run's limits. A reserve protects allowance for Lead; a task or reserve limit pauses child work rather than proving the task impossible. Budget changes do not resume a paused task: Lead explicitly resumes it after the limit or dependency is resolved. Token limits govern later request admission from observed receipts; an admitted request can finish beyond the allowance, and unavailable usage remains explicit.

Save reproducible arguments and exact code in RESEARCH.md and owned source files. In the final summary, identify what was established or remains open, the declarations or evidence to inspect, the precise blocker if any, and a useful next step. Do not report a rejected delivery as checked or a finished investigation as an accepted proof. No fixed report template or role acknowledgment is required.

### Shared research and saved findings

Lead can `publish` a concise current research summary describing the useful route, established findings with references, working assumptions, decisive negative evidence and remaining gaps. Choose the content needed by the team; no fixed report format is required. Publishing advances its revision, saves its publication time and sends members a `research-updated` event. The latest summary is included in member contexts and `poll`/`wait` snapshots with `advisory:true`. It conveys the team's current judgment and grants no proof acceptance. Members send corrections or disagreements directly to the relevant peers and Lead; Lead decides how to update the shared route and summary.

Contexts and `poll`/`wait` use a bounded overview: current/next/recent tasks and recent references, task-text previews and omission counts. Member `activity` distinguishes preparing, running, stopping, checking, integrating, paused and idle; these are execution observations, not mathematical progress. The budget projection reports shared remaining allowance, Lead reserve and, for a current member, its cumulative task use. Use `history` to page complete task objectives, summaries and errors and read the full current research summary. A task's `historyOffset` is its offset within that member's history. Without `agentId`, history follows the host's stable global task sequence. Follow `nextOffset` until null; the default limit is 4 and maximum 8, with a byte limit stopping at whole-task boundaries. Declaration arrays are omitted; use `inspect` for checked proof interfaces.

Context inbox events can be previews. `textTruncated` means the body remains unread; `previewThrough` is not a receive cursor. Use `poll(after: through)` to obtain complete messages. `poll`/`wait` return at most 8 complete events and can return fewer to stay within their byte allowance; continue from their returned cursor. Older omitted task and reference records remain in the archive and are discoverable through task history and evidence IDs.

After an executed task's writer has stopped, the host saves a stable `sourceCommit` and `researchArtifactId`. The artifact contains its summary and task status, plus RESEARCH.md when present; absent notes are reported explicitly. These references remain discoverable in task results and the team's `researchArtifacts` index when the producer takes another assignment. Use `inspect-research` with the artifact ID to read the saved findings at that fixed commit, following pagination until complete. This is the path for a source survey, counterexample argument, failed route or useful calculation without a formal Lean delivery. Refer to its hypotheses and evidence when using it; a stored artifact is advisory even if its note reports a successful local check. Inspect checked proof declarations separately with `inspect`.

Use the tool's `operation` field:

| Operation | Caller | Arguments and result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `spawn` | Lead | `agentId`, `message`, optional `budget:{tokens?,turns?}`: create a child and queue its first assignment; returns a task ID. |
| `assign` | Lead | `agentId`, `message`, optional `budget:{tokens?,turns?}`: queue follow-up work for an existing child. |
| `budget` | Lead | Optional `leadReserve:{tokens?,turns?}` and/or the complete `agentId`, `taskId`, `budget:{tokens?,turns?}` group: replace the reserve or that task's cumulative allowance without resuming it. |
| `yield` | Child | `reason`: save and return for confirmed writer shutdown, pausing the current task while retaining its identity and drafts. |
| `resume` | Lead | `agentId`, `taskId`: queue a paused task again with the same identity and cumulative allowance. |
| `history` | Any member | Optional `agentId`, `offset`, `limit` (default 4, maximum 8): page complete task text, source/evidence references and the full current advisory research summary. |
| `cancel` | Lead | `agentId`, `taskId`, optional `reason` and `replacement`: cancel a queued task or request a running task's shutdown; an optional replacement message queues a new task for the same member. |
| `publish` | Lead | `summary`: update the versioned advisory team research summary and notify members. |
| `message` | Any member | `agentId`, `message`, optional `relatedTaskId` and `researchRevision`: durably save an advisory message; native notification is queued separately. |
| `poll` | Any member | Optional `after` cursor: receive addressed events, the advisory research summary, worker/task results, research artifact references, delivery IDs and adoption status immediately. |
| `wait` | Any member | `after`, optional `timeoutMs` from 1 to 30000: wait for addressed events, then return the same snapshot as `poll`. |
| `inspect` | Any member | `deliveryId`: read a checked delivery's exact declarations, commit, source digest, imports, source excerpts and proof evidence. Excerpts report truncation. |
| `inspect-research` | Any member | `artifactId`, optional `offset` and `limit` in Unicode code points: read a page of saved research notes at their fixed commit; follow `nextOffset` until complete. |
| `submit` | Child | `summary`, `declarations`: nominate the current assignment's exact declarations for checking after returning. |
| `adopt` | Lead | `deliveryId`, optional `targetId` (default `lead`): queue checked integration into Lead or an idle child; returns before integration completes. |
| `stop` | Lead | `agentId`: prevent further assignments and request interruption of an active child; shutdown finishes asynchronously. |

`spawn` and `assign` queue work without waiting for its completion. Receive results through `poll` or `wait`, passing the cursor returned by either receive operation as `after` on the next one. Each result can arrive while other children continue. Messages and completion summaries are advisory. Use `inspect` to assess a proof delivery before deciding whether to adopt it. A saved result is available even after its producer receives another assignment.

A child submitting Lean work supplies 1–32 declarations, each with `file`, fully qualified `name` and complete closed `type`; optional `description` and explicit `premises` preserve its interface. List every changed declaration in the delivery. Preserve frozen statements and source ownership; an unchecked source change is not transferable evidence. Save sources and research notes, call `submit`, then return. Its `pending-writer-stop` response records the request; it does not assert that checking passed. The host confirms shutdown, freezes actual source bytes and checks the submitted declarations before publishing a delivery ID. Read failures from the task/event feedback and correct them through a later assignment.

Draft sources from a deferred or rejected delivery remain part of the next submission. List their changed declarations as well as the new corrections; only a successful delivery advances this source baseline. A stopped child may receive a checked peer adoption while retaining its own unfinished draft. The host preserves that draft and its delivery baseline, rechecks the integration and reports conflicts. Later submissions still list all of the child's own unpublished changes; adopted peer declarations are already tracked by the host and need not be manually resubmitted as new work.

For adoption into Lead, call `adopt` and return to release your writer. The host freezes current sources, checks the selected delivery against that version, integrates it, and continues the saved Lead thread with feedback. Adoption into a child requires that target to be idle, with no active writer, and returns a queued request. The host completes pending integration before dispatching its follow-up work, so Lead may queue the consumer assignment after requesting adoption. Read the adoption status and feedback before treating the declarations as available. Messages and cancellation can continue while the host checks that adoption. A conflict or failed recheck is reported to Lead. Do not copy a peer's mutable files or edit another worktree or host state to bypass this protocol.

Keep organizing useful work while children are active. On host recovery, an interrupted assignment retains its sources and thread but needs an explicit new assignment to continue. Pending checking and adoption retain their saved identities. An unconfirmed writer stop prevents further writing or acceptance in that workspace.

A checked local delivery or completed adoption does not establish the original problem. The host independently checks every frozen original target before whole-problem acceptance.

## Other delegation modes

When only `theoremos_investigate` is exposed, use its bounded `questions` array for read-only investigations. That call waits for all results; investigators do not retain proof-writing assignments. The primary researcher integrates any useful findings within its own source permissions.

Without a supported team interface, use read-only investigations or separately isolated candidate workspaces within the actual host capability. Do not grant broader permissions, edit run state or treat a completion message as proof acceptance. An unavailable child tool is not a reason to fabricate a parallel workflow; built-in spawn/fork remains disabled in these host-managed modes.
